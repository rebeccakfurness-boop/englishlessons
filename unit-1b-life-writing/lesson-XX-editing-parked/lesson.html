Cut, Sharpen, Vary, Check

Last lesson you planned and drafted the opening of your own true story. Today you do the thing that actually separates decent writing from good writing: you go back and make it better. Not by adding more, but by making every word earn its place.

Why this matters

Cambridge objective 8Wp.04 asks you to evaluate and edit to improve the accuracy and effectiveness of language, grammar and structure. Notice the two halves of that: effectiveness and accuracy. They are different jobs, and today you learn to do them in the right order.

Teach · The big distinction

Editing Is Not Proofreading

Most people use these two words to mean the same thing. They don't, and confusing them is the single most common reason a redraft doesn't improve anything.

Job one

Editing = effectiveness

Does this writing work? Does it land?

  • Cutting what isn't needed
  • Replacing vague words with precise ones
  • Changing sentence lengths
  • Moving or deleting whole sentences
Job two

Proofreading = accuracy

Is anything actually wrong?

  • Spelling
  • Punctuation
  • Tense consistency
  • Homophones (there / their / they're)

The order matters. Always edit first, proofread last. There is no point perfecting the punctuation of a sentence you are about to delete.

Teach · The method

The Four Lenses

Rather than reading your draft once and vaguely hoping to spot problems, read it four times, looking for one thing each time. Tap each lens to see what it means.

The first three lenses are editing. The fourth is proofreading. That's why Check comes last.

Now your turn

Four Passes Over Your Own Draft

Get your Lesson 10 draft in front of you. We'll go through it together, one lens at a time, and you mark your changes straight onto Worksheet 11 as we go.

PASS 1 · CUT

Read it through hunting only for words doing no work. Every very, really, quite and a lot. Any sentence that repeats an idea you've already made. Cross them out. Don't replace them yet.

PASS 2 · SHARPEN

Find every vague word and make it exact. How many people actually? What did it actually smell like? What did they actually say? Where you've told a feeling, try to show it instead.

PASS 3 · VARY

Read it aloud. Where do three sentences run at the same length? Find your most important moment and give it a short, blunt sentence. Check you haven't started four sentences in a row with "I".

PASS 4 · CHECK

Only now. Spelling, punctuation, and one tense all the way through. Read the final sentence first and work backwards, because it stops your brain skimming what it expects to see.

Reading backwards for the proofread is a genuine professional trick. Your eye stops autocorrecting the text when it can't follow the meaning.
