Fact or Opinion?

Both of these belong in life writing. A piece made only of reads like a police report. A piece made only of is just someone shouting. The skill is knowing which one you are writing.

Fact

Someone could check it

You could prove it right or wrong. It does not matter whether it is interesting, only whether it is checkable.

"The bus left at 6:15."
"Eleven houses burned."
Opinion

It is a judgement

It might be completely fair, and you might agree with it, but nobody can prove it. It belongs to the writer.

"The journey was far too long."
"Nobody did enough to help."

Watch out for these four. Obviously, clearly, everyone knows and of course. They usually mean an opinion is being dressed up to look like a fact. That is where starts.

Two Shapes for the Same Events

You have five true things to tell someone. The order you choose decides what kind of piece you are writing.

News articles, reports, appeals. Tells you everything straight away.
  1. A headline that gives the story in one line
  2. The single most important thing, immediately
  3. The other facts, most important first
  4. Description and background
  5. What the reader should do about it

Life writing, memoir, travel writing. Holds things back.
  1. A : one vivid moment, no explanation yet
  2. Detail building up, one piece at a time
  3. The : something shifts or surprises you
  4. What happened in the end
  5. What you understand now, looking back

The difference in one line. A report gives you the ending first, because it wants you to know. A story saves the ending, because it wants you to feel.

You have already used one of these. Your homework asked for a headline, then the most important thing, then facts, then description, then what people should do. That is the report shape. Now it has a name.

You Have Five, and You Use One

Almost everybody writes only what they saw. The other four senses are what stop a piece being a list of events and start putting the reader in the room. Tap each one for an example.

Smell is the strongest one. Out of all five senses, smell is the one most closely connected to memory. If you can remember what a place smelled like, that detail will almost always beat what it looked like.

Which Sense Is Doing the Work?

Eight details from real life writing. Name the sense before you tap, and notice how few of them are sight.

Read the sentence and ask: if I were actually there, which part of my body would notice this?

My eyes is sight. My ears is sound. My nose is smell. My mouth is taste. My skin is touch.

One of the eight is a trick. If you cannot picture anything at all, that is the trick one.
