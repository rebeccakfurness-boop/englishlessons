Two things to listen out for

1. Any moment where somebody states an opinion as though it were a fact. Those are the moments we practised spotting in our lesson.

2. Any moment where leaving something out changes the story, even though nothing untrue has been said. That is bias by selection, the same thing the two beach clean-up accounts were doing.

Fact or opinion?

B

Find the bias

Read this account of a school trip. Everything in it is true.

Our class went to the museum in Mataram last Thursday. The bus was supposed to leave at half past seven and did not leave until ten past eight. Twenty-eight of us went.

The guide talked for fifty minutes before we were allowed to look at anything, which felt like a very long time, and by then three people had already asked to sit down. We saw the textile collection and some old photographs. Obviously it would have been better to just give us the leaflet and let us walk round on our own. We got back at four.

Written for this lesson.

Write out two statements from the account that are facts.
Write out two statements that are opinions, and say which word in each one gives it away.
This is the important one. Name two true things the writer has probably left out, and explain how putting them in would change the impression the reader gets.
C

Same facts, two shapes

Here are five true things about the same event. You are going to open the same story twice, once in each shape.

1. A water pipe burst on the main road late on Tuesday night.

2. Pak Darman noticed the leak at about eleven o'clock while he was walking his dog.

3. Forty-two houses had no water for two days.

4. The village office handed out bottled water on Wednesday morning.

5. The repairs were finished on Thursday afternoon.

Write a headline, then the first sentence. The most important information comes straight away.
Now write the first two sentences as life writing, told by somebody who lives in one of the forty-two houses. Hold something back.
Which version makes a reader want to keep reading, and which is more useful to somebody in a hurry? Say why.
D

Add the missing senses

This paragraph uses only sight. Rewrite it in the box, keeping the same events, but adding at least one sound, one smell and one touch detail. Add a taste detail too if you can find a place for it.

I walked into the kitchen. My aunt was standing at the table. There was a big pot on the stove. The window was open and I could see the garden. She was wearing her green apron. There were vegetables on the board in front of her.
