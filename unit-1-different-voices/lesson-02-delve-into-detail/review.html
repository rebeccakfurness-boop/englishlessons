Read the story

💡 Words in this story that might be new

packed: put clothes and things into a bag, ready to travel.

zip: the part that opens and closes a bag.

the long way: a road that takes more time than you need. You choose it so the walk is longer.

bench: a long seat, often outside.

properly: carefully and completely.

ordinary: normal. Nothing special.

Prologue

The last week of term, three years ago.

Nobody at Harbour Road School knew that Priya had already packed. Her bag sat under her bed with the zip half open, and on top of everything, folded twice, was the map.

She walked to school the long way that morning, past the fish market, past the blue gate, past the bench where she and Amina had eaten lunch every day for four years. She wanted to remember all of it properly.

Amina was waiting at the gate, the way she always was.

"You're late," Amina said.

"I know," said Priya. And that was the last ordinary thing either of them said to each other for a very long time.

Be a detective

What to do: Now find the clues. Each question asks you to pick the best evidence. Evidence means the exact words from the story that prove your answer. Scroll up to read the story again whenever you want.

💡 Remind me how to pick the best evidence

More than one answer can feel right. You want the strongest one.

The strongest evidence is the one that proves your answer on its own, with no guessing.

Ask yourself: if I showed only these words to someone, would they agree with me?

Feelings in writing

💡 What do these feeling words mean?

Comfort: feeling safe and happy, because something is familiar.

Unease: feeling that something is not right, but you do not know why.

Excitement: feeling happy and full of energy about something.

Sorrow: a deep, quiet sadness.

Fear: feeling frightened.

Anger: feeling cross or furious.

Word parts

2 min Done

What to do: Long words are often made of small parts joined together. Choose the meaning of the part in bold.

💡 Show me all the word parts

pro = before  ·  epi = in addition  ·  logos = word or speech

mono = one  ·  auto = self  ·  trans = across

micro = small  ·  sub = under  ·  ology = the study of

So prologue is pro + logos. It means "the words before". That is why a prologue comes before Chapter 1.

💡 I am stuck. Help me start.

Try adding one small detail at the end. Do not say anyone is sad or scared.

You could try one of these beginnings:

"She got on the bus with both..."
"She got on the bus and did not..."
"She got on the bus. Nobody..."

👀 Show me one example answer

"She got on the bus with both suitcases, and she did not look back at the house."

No word in that sentence is frightening. Two suitcases and one small action do all the work. The reader builds the worry in their own head. That is inference, from the writer's side.
