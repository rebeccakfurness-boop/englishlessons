Short Line, Long Line

Two marks. They look almost the same, and they do completely opposite jobs. One glues words together. The other pulls them apart and makes your reader wait.

-
The hyphen

Short. It is glue. It sticks words together so they become one idea.

–
The dash

Longer. It is a gap. It holds the reader still, right before the good bit.

Last lesson you learned to show, not tell. Today you learn where to put the silence. A dash is how a writer says wait for it without using any words at all.

Teach · The glue

The Hyphen Sticks Things Together

A hyphen takes two or three separate words and turns them into one idea. Once they are glued, you read them as a single thing.

Look at what happens without the glue:

a ten year old girl   three separate words, a bit confusing

a ten-year-old girl   one idea, describing the girl

A number rule worth remembering: every number from twenty-one to ninety-nine is written with a hyphen. So is a fraction, like two-thirds.

Play · The gluer

Stick Them Together

Tap each pile of words and watch the hyphen snap them into one idea.

Notice there are no spaces around a hyphen. It is glue, so it sits tight against both words.

Teach · The gap

The Dash Makes You Wait

A dash is longer, and it has spaces around it. It is the opposite of glue. It opens a gap, and a reader falls into that gap for half a second.

A single dash can do three jobs:

A dramatic pause

Right before the big moment, or right before a disappointing one.

An aside

A little comment the writer leans in to say. Two dashes, one each side.

Extra information

Something added on the end, or a surprising contrast.

Play · The pause machine

Feel the Difference

Same sentence. One has a comma, one has a dash. Press play and watch how long the reader is made to wait.

Press play, then swap and press play again.

A comma is a breath. A dash is a held breath. The words never changed. Only how long you had to wait for the end.
