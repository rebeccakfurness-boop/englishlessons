Plan Your Prologue

Today we plan a story together. Not a practice one. Yours. We work out who is in it, where it happens, what your reader can see and hear and smell, and the one secret you are going to hide from them. By the end of the lesson the whole plan is on the screen. Then you go away and write it.

What we do today
  • Find your who, where and when
  • Collect a detail for all five senses
  • Build a character out of three things
  • Decide the secret you will not explain
  • Choose your voice, your mood and your shape
✍️ And afterwards

You take the plan home and write the prologue itself. Two or three paragraphs. You will not be staring at an empty page, because every decision will already be made.

Planning is the hard part. We are doing the hard part together.

Your Cambridge book puts it in one line: "plan, write and edit a prologue." Today is the planning. You have spent three lessons collecting tools, and now you get to use every single one of them.

Plan · The seed

Who, Where, When

Every story starts with these three. There are no wrong answers. If you get stuck, press Give me an idea and take one, or change it into something better.

Your teacher can type while you talk. Everything you say gets saved on this page.

Plan · The five senses

What Can We Sense?

This is the part that makes a reader feel like they are standing there. One detail for each sense. Not big dramatic things: small, true, exact ones.

You do not have to use all five when you write. Three is plenty. But collect five now, because then you get to choose the best ones instead of scraping around for any at all.

Plan · Your person

Build Your Character

Remember the character machine? Same three questions, except this time the answers are yours and they go into your real story.

Remember: not one of these is allowed to say how your character feels. Show the reader, and let them work it out.

Plan · The good bit

Your Secret

A prologue works because of something it does not tell you. This is the thing your reader will chase. You know it. They do not. Yet.

The rule you learned as a detective, now backwards. Do not explain your secret. Plant one small clue that points at it and then walk away. If your reader has to work, they will keep reading.

Plan · Voice and mood

Two Quick Decisions

These two choices change how your whole story sounds. Pick one from each row.

Who is telling it?
First person puts the reader inside your character. Third person lets you tell them things your character does not know.
What should the reader feel?
Choose the mood now and every word choice afterwards gets easier, because you know what you are aiming at.
Plan · The shape

What Happens, In What Order

Three short steps. This is your whole prologue, and it is only two or three paragraphs, so it does not need much.
