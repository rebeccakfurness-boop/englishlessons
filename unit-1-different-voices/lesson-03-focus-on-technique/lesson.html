Teach · Trick one

Who Is Telling It?

Every story is told by somebody. That somebody is the narrator, and a writer gets to choose where they stand.

First person

Uses I, me, my. You are inside the character's head, seeing only what they see.

"I pushed the gate open."

Third person

Uses she, he, they. You are outside, watching. You can be told things the character does not know.

"She pushed the gate open."

Remember the all-knowing narrator? That is third person doing its special trick. "What she did not know was..." only works from outside. In first person it is impossible, because if she does not know it, she cannot tell you.

Play · The swapper

Flip the Whole Story

Same events. Same words, almost. Press the buttons and watch what changes, then tell me which one feels closer to you.

Only five words changed. Nothing else moved at all. But one version feels like watching through a window, and the other feels like being the person carrying the box. That is how much work a pronoun does.

Teach · Trick two

The Loud Word

When you speak, you make one word louder to change what you mean. Writers cannot be loud on paper, so they lean the word over instead. That is what italics are for.

Italics have two jobs in a story:

1. Say it louder

Push on one word so the reader hears it the way you do.

2. Change the voice

Show that a different person is speaking now, like the note in our prologue last lesson.

Play · The loud word game

Tap any word above to make it loud.

Nothing about the sentence changed. Not one letter. Only which word you leaned on.

Teach · Trick three

Show, Do Not Tell

This is the biggest one, and it is the reason your reader believes you.

TELLING

"She was angry."

You have given the reader a label. They read it and move on. They feel nothing.

SHOWING

"She put the cup down harder than she meant to."

Now the reader works it out, and because they worked it out, they believe it.

Notice this is inference again, from the other side. Last lesson you found the clues. Today you are the one hiding them.
